## About the mailbox passwords — the plan changed, and for the better

The Aug 5 plan was: share the mailbox passwords, turn 2FA off. **That plan can't
be carried out any more.** Google stopped accepting ordinary passwords for this
kind of connection on 14 March 2025. It isn't unwise now, it simply doesn't
work — the mailbox refuses the login.

The usual replacement is an "app password", which is what I recommended on
Aug 6. That turned out to have a catch: **an app password requires 2-Step
Verification to be switched on**, which is the exact thing you'd asked to avoid.
The first step of the plan would have made the second step impossible.

So I've used the third route, which is better than either: **a private
connection registered to your own Workspace**. You signed in once, in your own
browser, and clicked Allow. That's it — no password was shared, no 2-Step
Verification was needed, and nothing has to be repeated.

**And it asked for read-only access, which is the part that matters.**

The permission it holds is *"read your email messages and settings"* and nothing
else. Not "read and delete". Not "read and send". So three of the promises I
made you in the specification stopped being promises:

| I said it would never... | Now enforced by |
|---|---|
| delete an email | Google refuses — the permission cannot delete |
| mark your mail as read | it cannot set flags; your unread count is untouched |
| touch your labels or folders | it cannot write any label |

That's a stronger guarantee than me writing careful code. **A mistake in my
software can't reach past a permission it was never given.** If the day comes
that you want it drafting replies or applying labels, that needs a wider
permission and it's a conversation, not a switch I can flip quietly — there's a
check in the build that stops it happening by accident.

You can see and revoke it any time at
**myaccount.google.com → Security → Your connections to third-party apps**.

**Still needed from you here:** the four Shared Drives — MRECAI, C.H. Shink,
Atlase, MLECA. That's where business documents get filed once we're past your
own Mac, and there's nowhere to put anything until they exist.

**Still true:** the five passwords in the setup document should be changed. Not

mail connection above doesn't depend on them, so this is no longer blocking

## Something about your domain, unrelated to the software

**`mrecai.com` doesn't publish a DMARC record.**

In plain terms: there's nothing telling other mail servers what to do with a
message that *claims* to be from `matthew@mrecai.com` but isn't. Anyone can send
mail as you, and the receiving server has no instruction to reject it.

I noticed because the system checks this on every message, and yours came back
blank.

The reason I'm raising it rather than filing it away: **look at what's actually
in your mailbox this week.** A State Farm policy thread, a mortgage servicing
change, an escrow analysis. That's precisely the correspondence that payment
redirection fraud targets — the fake "our bank details have changed" email that
goes to a client or a title company, apparently from you, a week before a
closing.

It's one DNS record, and whoever manages your domain can add it in ten minutes.
Start it in observe-only mode so nothing of yours gets blocked while you watch
what it reports. Worth checking `atlase.ai` and `mleca.com` at the same time.

Not urgent this week. Worth doing this month.

### 2. **New —** where do vehicle documents go?

Your GEICO insurance card and the 2022 Toyota signature page both came to your
**business** address. Your own rule — which I think is the right one — is that
the address a document arrives at decides whose it is. So they filed under
MRECAI.

But MRECAI has no category for vehicles. The filing system has one under
*personal* and not under *business*, so both ended up in **CLIENTS**, which is
wrong and will keep being wrong.

Two ways to fix it, and it's your call:

- **Add a VEHICLES category to the businesses.** Right if the cars are business
  vehicles, insured and expensed through the company.
- **Make vehicle documents personal regardless of which address they arrive
  at.** Right if they're your cars and the business address is just where the
  post happens to land.
