## What changed since Tuesday

**It reads real mail now.** Photographs, scanned PDFs, and PDFs that arrive as
proper documents — each by a different route, because OCR'ing a PDF that
already contains text *introduces* mistakes into something that was already
perfect. Anything it can't read is set aside and flagged, never guessed at.

**There's a to-do list and a morning brief**, as above.

**There are backups, and I've restored one.** Nightly, encrypted, and I've
proved a restore actually works rather than assuming it. That distinction
matters more than it sounds: the most common way a backup fails is by looking
fine and being empty, and you find out on the day you need it.

**223 automated checks**, up from 42 on Tuesday, all passing on your machine.

I also found and fixed eleven defects, all of them by running real documents
through and reading what came out — not one was caught by the tests. Two were
the kind worth mentioning: a rule you'd asked for (jury duty always goes to
legal) was written down in a settings file that no part of the program actually
read, and the safety rules that stop it deleting or sending anything were being
"verified" by a test that only checked the settings file said the right words.
Both are now enforced properly and can't quietly stop working.

You mentioned the array has more than one volume. Tell me which one isn't the
same physical disk as the documents and I'll point backups at it.

### 8. **New —** do you want a copy kept off-site?

A backup in your office doesn't survive theft or fire.

The catch is real, which is why I'm asking rather than deciding: an off-site

encrypts everything before it's sent, so the provider only ever holds
scrambled data and the key never leaves your Mac — that's a genuine
protection, not a technicality. But it's your data and your clients, so it's
your call, and I'd want it in writing either way.

There's also a **password for the backups** now, stored on the Mac. Put a copy
in your password manager. If it's ever lost, every backup becomes permanently
unreadable — same as the FileVault key, no back door.
